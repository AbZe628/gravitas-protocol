A guide for Shariah boards and the banks they serve

What Majlis is, what it does,
and how to use it

Majlis is where a Shariah board does its work. A question arrives from the bank. The board reads it, argues about it, votes on it, and writes down why. Majlis keeps all of that in one place, so anyone can find the decision a year later and check what it was based on.

This guide explains every part of it. It is written for two readers: a scholar who will use it, and a bank officer who needs to know what it does before agreeing to use it.

Majlis does not decide anything.

It does not say whether a product is permissible. It does not choose a threshold, a method, or a standard. Every one of those is the board's, and the software is built to refuse them. What Majlis does is hold the work, do the arithmetic, and show its own gaps.

1. Who uses Majlis

Five kinds of people sign in. Each one sees a different set of buttons. Majlis hides what a person may not do, and the server refuses it as well, so a hidden button is not the only thing stopping them.

RoleWhat they can do
SignatoryA scholar who signs Reads everything. Takes part in discussion. Votes. Objects during a waiting period. Closes a vote once enough signatures are in.
Advisory memberA scholar without a signature Reads everything and takes part in discussion. Their written view is kept in the record. It does not count towards the number of signatures needed.
Technical liaisonThe engineer or product person Explains how something works when the board asks. Records the steps that belong to the bank. No vote.
InstitutionThe desk with a question Puts a question to the board. Reads what happened to its own questions. Sees nothing else. This is the account you give to a product team or a compliance officer.
ObserverAn auditor or a regulator Reads. Writes nothing.

One person can also hold an office as well as a role. The chair calls meetings. The secretary writes down what the bank did, such as filing a plan to fix a problem. An office does not give anyone more authority to decide. It only says who does the paperwork.

3. The path a question takes

This is the main thing to understand. A question goes through eight steps from the desk that asks it to the document the bank files. Each step happens on one screen, and each one leaves a record.

  1. The bank asks

    Someone at the bank signs in with an institution account and writes the question in their own words. They say what they are waiting to do, so the board can see which questions are holding up work.

    The question is never edited. What the board thinks the question is becomes a separate text, written by a named member. The two sit side by side, so you can see whether the board answered what was asked.

    Screen: the first screen for an institution account. Or Questions for the board.

  2. The board picks it up

    The board sees a queue, oldest first. A member either opens the question as a matter, or declines it and says why. The reason is required. Without one the bank cannot tell whether to come back with more information or not to come back at all.

    A declined question can be reconsidered later. Both decisions stay in the record.

    Screen: Questions

  3. The board sets out the question

    A matter holds the question, what actually happens in the transaction, and what is not being decided. That last part matters. It stops a narrow approval being read a year later as a broad one.

    Screen: the matter itself

  4. The board judges it against a contract shape

    Majlis ships nineteen contract shapes with the conditions each one is normally held to. Murabaha, ijara, mudaraba, sukuk, and so on. The board answers each condition in its own words: met, not met, or does not apply.

    These conditions are a draft. They bind nobody until this board adopts them. Where the board has ruled on the same shape before, its earlier answers are offered under each condition, and accepting one records it as your finding today.

    Screen: the matter, lower down. Or More › Library to read the shapes.

The path a question takes continued

  1. The board sets the operative terms

    A term is a name, a number, a unit, and a sentence saying what it does. For example: minTangibleRatioBps, 5100, basis points, tangible assets must be at least 51% of pool value.

    The plain sentence is required. It is what the scholar actually reads. It is left out of the fingerprint on purpose, so improving the wording never cancels an approval.

    Screen: the matter

  2. The board votes

    Each signatory records a position and a reason. The reason is required. A list of names with no reasons cannot be followed the next time a similar question comes up.

    Permitting something needs the full number of signatures and then waits 48 hours before it takes effect. Any one member can object during that time. Restricting something takes effect at once on fewer signatures, and then has to be confirmed by the full board or it falls away.

    Screen: the matter, under What is next

  3. Majlis writes the ruling

    When the vote closes and the waiting period ends, Majlis assembles the document: the question, what happens, what was not decided, the terms, and every member's reasoning next to their position. It opens in a browser tab and saves as a PDF.

    Screen: the matter, under Where this ruling ends

  4. Majlis drafts the clauses

    From the same ruling, Majlis writes out the clauses an agreement has to contain. Every clause comes from something a member wrote: a condition they found, a term they approved, something they held outside the question.

    This is not a contract and it is not legal advice. It covers only what the board ruled on. A financing agreement contains much more, and the rest was never put to the board. The draft says so at the top, above the clauses.

    Screen: the matter, next to the ruling

4. What happens after a ruling

Most compliance is not lost when a board rules badly. It is lost afterwards, when the transactions drift away from what was approved and nobody compares the two. Majlis has four ways of catching that.

Drift

A pool that was 51% tangible in March is 47% in July because it rebalanced. Nobody did anything wrong. Majlis compares the composition against the terms the board set and raises the question.

It never re-rules. The status stays what the board made it.

More › Register

Examinations

The bank's own review function checks a sample of executed transactions against the ruling, and records what it found: how many were looked at, which conditions held, and how many exceptions there were.

Majlis does not choose the sample. The examiner does, and says how.

Events

The bank reports a breach. The board decides whether it was real, now rather than next quarter. From that moment thirty days run for a plan to fix it. Nine steps follow, and four of them belong to the bank.

More › Events

Review dates

A ruling comes back to the board on an interval the board sets. The dates are worked out from the rules themselves. You can subscribe to them from your own calendar.

5. The arithmetic Majlis will do

Six calculations, under More › Calculations. Every one of them shows the full sum. None of them reaches a verdict.

ScreeningThree ratios on a company's figures. The board sets every limit. Majlis ships none. Send no limit and the ratio is worked out and reported untested.
PurificationHow much income must be given away, and to where.
ZakatOn the base and by the method the board chooses.
Profit distributionWhat depositors are paid, and what the reserves did to it.
TradabilityWhere a pool falls against the bands this board set, quoted in the board's own words.
Late paymentAn increase charged on a late payment, and where it goes. You say first whether the debtor could have paid.

Money is never handled as a decimal number in the usual programming sense. Every amount stays exact, and every comparison is done by multiplying rather than dividing, so a figure that displays as 30.00% can never be inside the limit on one screen and outside it on another.

6. The documents it produces

Each one opens in a browser tab and saves as a PDF. Nothing is generated in advance. They are built from the record each time they are asked for, so they cannot fall out of step with it.

7. With a chain, and without one

Majlis works the same either way. The difference is only what happens to the ruling at the end.

Without a chain

The ruling is a document

Everything runs inside the application: the question, the evidence, the vote, the file. Majlis produces the PDF, and somebody at the bank sends it on.

Majlis does not send it, and says so on the screen. That step is a person's.

With a policy registry

The terms are read before every transaction

The same board, the same record, the same vote. Instead of a document to send, the terms are written where they are consulted. A transaction that would break one does not go through.

The document still exists, as the account of the decision rather than the instrument of it.

A bank with no chain anywhere near it is running the ordinary installation, not a reduced one. The day it connects a registry, nothing about how the board works changes.

8. What Majlis will not do

This section matters as much as the rest. A bank should know the limits before it relies on the product, not after.

  • It does not rule. No screen and no document says whether something is permissible. The assistant refuses the question in three languages, and so does the guide.
  • It follows no standard and ships none. There is no threshold, no ratio and no citation built in. Which standard governs is your board's decision. The only place a standard is ever named is where your board writes it, in its own words.
  • It does not sign. A decision recorded in Majlis does not change the policy registry by itself. Signing authority moving into Majlis is the next stage of the work and is not built.
  • It does not send anything. No email leaves the building unless your institution wires up its own mail relay. Where none is configured, Majlis writes the words for a person to send and says on the screen that nobody has been told.
  • It does not choose an audit sample. Your review function chooses, and says how it chose.
  • It does not fill in gaps. Where your board has not decided something, every screen and every document says so rather than leaving a blank space that reads as a clean result.

10. Trying it

You will be sent a link and one account for each role. Sign in and work through the path in section 3. It takes about fifteen minutes.

  1. Sign in as the institution account. Put a question to the board. Say what you are waiting to do.
  2. Sign out. Sign in as a signatory. Open Questions. Your question is at the top of the queue.
  3. Take it up as a matter. Write the board's own version of the question.
  4. Choose a contract shape. Answer two or three of its conditions.
  5. Set one operative term. Give it a plain sentence.
  6. Open the vote. Record a position and a reason. Do the same with a second signatory account.
  7. Close the vote. Open the ruling, then the draft clauses.
  8. Sign back in as the institution account and look at your own question. It now names the matter it became.
Accounts

Accounts are generated for each pilot and sent separately from this document. They are never written in a file that travels by email.

Each one is a name and a password. There is no self service sign up, and no account can be created from inside the application, because an account is a person the board has agreed to.

11. Questions a bank usually asks

Does it replace our Shariah board?
No. It holds their work. Every judgement stays theirs, and the software refuses to make one.
Does it tell us what is halal?
No, and it is built so that it cannot. Ask it and it will explain where the question goes instead.
Which standard does it follow?
None. Your board decides that and writes it down in its own words. There is no threshold or citation built into the product.
Can we change our mind about something we decided?
Yes. Nothing is edited or deleted. A correction is written as a new entry that supersedes the old one, and both stay visible.
Who can see our questions?
Each institution's record is separated at the storage layer, not by a check in each screen. Within one bank, a desk sees only the questions it asked itself.
Do we need a blockchain?
No. Most banks will run it with nothing attached, and that is the ordinary installation.
What happens if we stop using it?
One action exports the whole record with a fingerprint. It is your record, and it leaves in a form you can read without us.
Is our data used to train anything?
No. The assistant is off unless you turn it on, and the in application guide answers only from what the software knows about itself, so nothing you type there leaves the building.

Everything in this guide describes what the software does today. Where something is designed and not built, this document says so, in the same words the application uses on the screen.
