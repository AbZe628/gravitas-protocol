# Gravitas Majlis

A handbook for the board, the secretariat and the bank.

Majlis is where a Shariah board does its work and keeps its record. The bank
asks a question. The board answers it against the conditions of a named
contract. The answer comes out as a document the bank can file, cite and be
audited against.

This handbook covers every screen and every tool. Each section says what you
see, what you press, and what happens.

---

## Why this exists

A Shariah board decides what a bank may and may not do. Today most of them
do it with email, spreadsheets and a folder of PDFs.

**The problem is not that boards decide badly. It is that nothing holds the
decision.** A ruling is written once and then lives in an attachment. The
conditions attached to it are read by whoever remembers them. A review date
sits in someone's calendar until that person leaves. A pool that was 51%
tangible when the board approved it drifts to 47% and nobody finds out until
the audit. A desk that wants to know whether it may sign an agreement asks a
scholar by email and waits nine weeks.

**Majlis holds all of it in one record.** The question arrives from the bank
with the contract attached. The board judges it against the conditions of a
named contract type, one condition at a time, each answer carrying the name
of the member who gave it and the reason they gave. The vote needs the
number of signatures the board's own constitution requires. What comes out
is a document, and the conditions the agreement failed come back as clauses
it has to add.

**What the bank gets** is three screens: what it asked and what became of
it, what binds it today, and what it still owes. No email thread, no
attachment, no waiting to be told what was decided six months ago.

**What the board gets** is its own record: every ruling, every reason,
every position, searchable, with the review dates and the drifting numbers
watched for it. And an audit export that answers, for any date, what was
permitted, who approved it, on what reasoning, and who dissented.

> Majlis never decides anything. Every calculation shows its arithmetic and
> stops; every reading quotes the contract and stops. The ruling is the
> board's, in the board's words, and the software's job is to make sure that
> nothing else is ever mistaken for it.

## 1 · How it works, in one page

1. **The bank asks.** Someone at the bank fills in four boxes and attaches
   the contract. The contract is read on the bank's own computer; the file
   never leaves it.
2. **The board takes it up.** A member turns the question into a matter and
   writes how the board will put it.
3. **The board picks the contract type.** Murabaha, ijara, sukuk and
   sixteen others. Picking one loads its conditions as the steps of the work.
4. **The board answers each condition.** Met, not met, or does not apply.
   Every answer needs a reason, and the reason is signed with the member's
   name.
5. **The board argues and votes.** Each vote carries a reason. A ruling
   needs the number of votes the board's own rules require.
6. **The bank gets the document.** Every condition, every answer, every
   reason. Conditions marked *not met* come back as clauses the agreement
   has to add.

The whole path takes about ten minutes to demonstrate and is written out
step by step in section 7.

## 2 · The parts of every screen

Once you know these five things, every screen is familiar.

![The board's home screen](guide/10-pocetna.jpg)

### The list on the left

Eight places, in three groups.

| group | where it takes you |
|---|---|
| **The work** | What needs you · Events · Coming |
| **What stands** | Rulings · The record · Search |
| **What we hold** | The register · The contract library |

The bank sees a shorter list of its own. Section 4.

### The bar along the top

Your name, search, the language (English, Arabic, Urdu), and a button that
is on every screen: **Ask the board**. The bell next to your name lights up
when the record has changed since you last looked.

### The tools down the right

Seven buttons, always in the same place: screening, purification, zakat,
profit distribution, tradability, late payment, and the list of everything
that has been worked out so far. One press opens the tool over the page you
are on. Section 8.

### The bar along the bottom

Four short facts about this installation. They are there so nobody has to
guess why a button is missing.

> Nothing here signs on the board's behalf · Terms are carried out
> elsewhere · No assistant is configured · Nobody outside is told

If the bank has connected email, the same bar says *Notices are sent*
instead. If it has connected the registry, it says *Terms are read before
every transaction*.

### The confirm window

Nothing is saved until you confirm it in a window. The window tells you
three things first: what the button does, what it means, and what happens
afterwards. If the server refuses, the refusal appears in the same window.

## 3 · Who can do what

There are five kinds of user. A board member can also hold an office:
chair or secretary.

| | signatory | advisory | liaison | observer | bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read the record | yes | yes | yes | yes | its own |
| Ask a question | yes | yes | yes | — | yes |
| Take a question up, answer conditions, argue | yes | yes | yes | — | — |
| **Vote** | yes | — | — | — | — |
| Record what the bank did | — | — | yes | — | yes |
| Keep the minutes | chair or secretary only | | | | |

- **Signatory** — votes. A ruling needs the number of signatories the
  board's own rules require. On the demonstration board that is three of
  five.
- **Advisory** — takes part in everything except the vote.
- **Liaison** — the technical member. Can also record things the bank did.
- **Observer** — reads everything, changes nothing.
- **Bank** — the institution's own desk, with its own three screens.

A password tells Majlis who you are. It is not a signature. Signing a
ruling with a scholar's own key is a later stage of the product, and the bar
at the bottom of every screen says so until it exists.

## 4 · The bank

The bank gets its own application, not the board's with half of it greyed
out. Three screens.

![The bank's screen](guide/D26-banka-sta-je-bilo.jpg)

### Asking a question

Four boxes:

| box | what goes in it |
|---|---|
| One line to find it by | a short title |
| What you are asking | the question itself. It is never edited by anyone |
| What the board needs to know | background |
| What you are waiting to do | what is held up while you wait |

Then attach the contract. Press **Choose a file** and pick the PDF. Majlis
reads the words out of it on your own computer and shows how many
characters it found. The file itself is not uploaded anywhere.

If the PDF is a scan, Majlis says so and will not take it. A scan has no
text behind it, so reading it would produce a list saying every condition is
missing, which would be wrong.

Press **Put it to the board**, then confirm in the window. The question
appears in the list below, marked **WAITING**.

Each question in that list shows where it got to:

- **WAITING** — with the board. You can still withdraw it.
- **TAKEN UP** — the board is working on it. There is nothing to read yet,
  and the row says so.
- **DECLINED** — with the board's reason, in full.

When the board has decided, the same row offers **the ruling, written up**.
That is the document itself.

### What binds you

Every ruling in force today, written for someone who has to follow it: the
condition, the figures, and a link to check a contract against it.

![What binds you](guide/30-sto-me-vezuje.jpg)

### What you may deal in

The same information from the other side: the instruments your desk may
use, and the condition attached to each.

![What you may deal in](guide/32-smijem-li.jpg)

### What you owe

Where the board found that something went wrong: what money is owed, to
whom, and which steps are still open.

![What you owe](guide/31-sto-dugujem.jpg)

### Check a contract

Attach a draft and see, condition by condition, where the agreement answers
it and where it does not, with the passage quoted. Nothing is uploaded here
either.

A ruling that has passed the vote but is still in its waiting period does
not appear under *What binds you* yet. The document says on its front page
when it takes effect.

## 5 · The secretariat

### Taking a question in for someone else

Most questions arrive by email from someone who will never have a login.
The secretary types it in using the same form, with one extra box: **who
actually asked**. The record shows that it was entered on their behalf.

### Sorting the queue

![The queue](guide/60-tajnik-red.jpg)

Oldest first. Two buttons on each question: **Take it up as a matter**, or
**Do not take it up**. Declining needs a reason, and that reason is the
only thing the bank gets back.

### Calling a sitting

![Sittings](guide/61-tajnik-sjednice.jpg)

Majlis does not host the call. It keeps the record of it.

- An agenda is required. Without one the button refuses and says why.
- Mark who attended. Quorum is worked out from that.
- **The board book** collects everything for the sitting into one document
  to send out beforehand.
- The chair or the secretary keeps the minutes.
- Closing the sitting puts the minutes into the record.

### Recording what the bank did

A liaison, or a member holding the secretary's office, can record something
the bank did: a plan filed, a payment made, a step delivered. The record
shows who entered it and on whose behalf.

## 7 · Working through a question

This is the main screen of the product.

![A matter](guide/D12-predmet-sa-oblikom.jpg)

The strip along the top is the whole job: **i** the brief, **01–06** the
conditions, **V** the vote. One step fills the middle of the screen. The
panels sit on the right. The buttons are along the bottom and never move.
Left and right arrow keys move between steps. The step is part of the web
address, so you can send a colleague the exact step rather than the matter.

A matter as it first opens, before anyone has chosen a contract type:

![A matter, just opened](guide/50-predmet.jpg)

### The order of work

**1. Open it for deliberation.** Until then only its author has to read it.
From here it appears on every member's list.

**2. Choose the contract type.** A question from the bank arrives without
one, because nobody has decided yet what kind of contract it is. The screen
says so and shows the library. Choosing a type loads its conditions as the
steps.

**3. Answer each condition.** Three buttons: **Met**, **Not met**, **Does
not apply**. A reason is required; the button stays dead until you write
one. *Not met* is also written into the contract clauses the bank gets
back, with a line saying the agreement has to provide for it.

**4. Argue it.** *What was said* is the discussion. You can name a
colleague, which puts it on their list.

**5. Vote.** The vote will not open until something has been said. Each
position needs a reason. Where dictation is switched on, a member can speak
the reason instead of typing it.

**6. Close the vote.** What happens next follows from the count and from
whether the ruling permits or restricts something.

### The panels on the right

**Guidance** — the question in the board's words and in the bank's, side by
side.

![Guidance](guide/51-predmet-guidance.jpg)

**What was said** — the discussion, with replies, and a button to ask a
named colleague.

![What was said](guide/52-predmet-sto-je-receno.jpg)

**Sources** — standards, earlier rulings, documents and the draft itself,
each with the relevant passage quoted. A member can mark a passage and write
a note against it.

![Sources](guide/53-predmet-izvori.jpg)

**Limits** — what this decision will not decide. Without this a ruling gets
read as wider than the board meant it.

![Limits](guide/54-predmet-granice.jpg)

**What the committee found** — if the matter was sent to a committee first.

### Changing an answer

Record the same condition again with a different answer. The earlier one
stays in the history, marked as superseded. Nothing is deleted.

### A decided matter

![A decided matter](guide/55-predmet-na-snazi.jpg)

The same screen, with the work behind it: what was decided, the documents it
produced, and — if the registry is connected — whether what is on the chain
still matches.

## 8 · The tools

Seven buttons on every screen. Six of them are calculators.

![The tools](guide/18-alati.jpg)

All six work the same way. You type the figures, they show the arithmetic,
and they stop there. None of them says whether something is permitted; that
is the board's answer, not the software's. The working is always shown, so
anyone can check it or disagree with it. Money is handled as exact decimals
throughout, never as rounded numbers.

### Screening

![Screening](guide/40-alat-screening.jpg)

Three ratios, measured against the limits **the board sets**:

- interest-bearing debt,
- cash and interest-bearing securities,
- non-permissible income.

You also choose what to divide by — market capitalisation or total assets.
Each ratio comes back with its top line, its bottom line, the limit it was
compared against, and whether it is over.

### Purification

![Purification](guide/41-alat-purification.jpg)

What has to be given away from a holding that passed screening and still
carries some non-permissible income.

There are three methods and they give different answers: per share, per
dividend, per net asset value. You pick; the result says which was used.

This is not the same as purification after a breach. That one comes out of
the bank's ledger and has a thirty-day clock on it. Section 15.

### Zakat

![Zakat](guide/42-alat-zakat.jpg)

Three choices, all of them the board's:

- **The base** — net assets, or net invested funds. On the same balance
  sheet these do not have to agree.
- **The year** — 2.5% for a lunar year, 2.577% for a solar one.
- **Who pays** — the bank, the shareholders, or both. The annual report
  asks for this.

### Profit distribution

![Profit distribution](guide/43-alat-distribution.jpg)

What the investment account holders receive. Two reserves, at rates and caps
the board approves, applied in a fixed order:

- **PER** comes out of gross profit, before the split, so the bank and the
  depositors both carry it.
- **IRR** comes out after the split, from the depositors' share only.

The order is shown because the annual report has to state whether the split
followed what the board approved.

### Tradability

![Tradability](guide/44-alat-tradability.jpg)

Whether a pool can be traded at market price or only at par depends on what
sits behind the paper: tangible assets, debts, cash, receivables. The tool
gives you the mix and the tangible ratio, and names which rules each
proportion falls under. Where the line is drawn is the board's ruling.

### Late payment

![Late payment](guide/45-alat-late.jpg)

What is charged when a debtor pays late, and where it goes. The charge goes
to charity. The bank keeps only what it can show it actually spent on
collection, and only if the board allows that at all. There is no field for
turning the charge into income.

### What has been worked out

![What has been recorded](guide/46-alat-recorded.jpg)

Every calculation the board has recorded, newest first, with its working,
who recorded it, and under which ruling. Any member can record one. Any
member can withdraw one, with a reason, in their own name.

Recording a figure is not approving it, and every record says so.

### Tools inside a step

When a condition needs a figure, Majlis opens the right tool inside that
step and files the result against that condition. The panel tells you which
of the two is happening, so nobody works out a figure and assumes it was
saved.

### Figures out of the contract

If a draft has been attached, the reader offers the numbers it found, each
with the passage it came from, so nobody retypes six figures off a balance
sheet. A number of the wrong kind is shown but cannot be used: *1.5% per
month* appears beside a money box with its quote, and will not go into it.

### One recorded figure

![One calculation](guide/94-izracun.jpg)

Every recorded figure has its own address, so a notice mentioning an amount
can point at the working instead of just stating a number.

## 9 · Counters and clocks

Nothing here is a stored list of reminders. Every count is worked out from
the record at the moment you look, so it cannot go stale.

| counter | where | what it says |
|---|---|---|
| What needs you | home screen and top bar | how much is waiting **on you**, and how much of it is late |
| Days here | every row in the queue | how long this has been waiting |
| Pace | the queue and the record | how long matters take, counted to the decision rather than to the effect |
| The count | the vote | for, against, abstained, the number required, and who has not voted yet, by name |
| Quorum | a sitting | worked out from who attended |
| Waited | a question from the bank | how long the bank has been waiting |
| The bell | the top bar | one number that goes up whenever the record changes |
| Answered | a matter | how many conditions have an answer, out of how many. No percentage and no progress bar |

## 11 · What Majlis watches on its own

Four things go looking without being asked. All four report; none of them
changes a ruling.

**Drift.** The board approves a pool that is 51% tangible. Months later it
has rebalanced to 47%, and the ruling now describes something that no longer
exists. Drift notices the difference and raises it. The status of the
holding stays whatever the board made it.

**Review.** A review date is the only deadline in a board's work that
nothing external pushes. Majlis compares the date to today and asks.

**Lapsed deadlines.** A restriction passed on a reduced quorum only stands
if the full board ratifies it in time. Majlis marks it as lapsed when the
window closes, instead of leaving a rule standing that nobody voted for.

**Examinations.** What was actually executed, compared with what the board
approved.

![Examinations](guide/23-provjere.jpg)

The screen lists the differences. There is no score and no pass mark; the
conclusion is the board's.

## 12 · The documents

Everything below opens, prints, and is built from the record rather than
typed by hand.

| document | what is in it | who can read it |
|---|---|---|
| The ruling | the question, every condition and its answer, who answered and why, the votes, the limits, and — while it is in its waiting period — the date it takes effect | the board, and the bank that asked |
| The contract clauses | a draft agreement built from what the board decided, with a clause for every condition marked *not met* | the board and the bank |
| The board book | everything for one sitting, in one document | the board |
| The compliance manual | every approved product and the conditions on it | the board and the bank |
| The annual report | the board's report to shareholders | the board |
| The audit export | what was permitted on a given date, who approved it, on what reasoning, with any dissent | the board |
| The matter pack | one matter with everything needed to decide it | the board |
| The dossier | everything the board ever decided about one holding | the board |
| Disclosure | what the bank still owes and still has to do | the bank |

![The board book](guide/93-knjiga-sjednice.jpg)

A ruling gets its reference number when it comes into force, not when the
question arrives. Most questions never become rulings, and numbering them
all would leave gaps that look like missing decisions.

## 13 · Reading a contract

![Checking a draft](guide/96-provjera-ugovora.jpg)

Attach or paste a draft and Majlis goes through it condition by condition.
For each one it reports one of three things:

- **found** — the draft deals with this, and here is the passage;
- **unclear** — something was found but it cannot be relied on;
- **absent** — nothing in the draft deals with it.

*Found* means the agreement addresses the condition. Whether it does so well
enough is the board's decision.

There are two readers. The plain one works on the words alone: no model, no
key, no network. The other uses a language model, where the bank has
configured one; it pulls out passages and quotes them, and a quote that
cannot be matched back to the text is downgraded to *unclear*. Which one ran
is written on the reading.

## 14 · The contract library

![The library](guide/17-biblioteka.jpg)

Nineteen contract types in ten families, with 89 conditions between them.

| family | types |
|---|---|
| Sale | murabaha (including commodity murabaha and tawarruq) · musawama · salam · istisna' |
| Lease | ijara · ijara muntahia bittamleek |
| Partnership | mudaraba · musharaka · diminishing musharaka |
| Agency | investment wakala |
| Securities | sukuk |
| Exchange | sarf |
| Security and transfer | kafala · rahn · hawala · wa'd |
| Interest-free lending | qard hasan |
| Insurance | takaful |
| Combination | combining contracts |

![Reading a draft against a type](guide/70-biblioteka-citanje.jpg)

### They arrive as drafts

Until the board does something with a contract type, its conditions are a
starting point and bind nobody. Every screen that shows them says so.

The board can adopt one as it stands, change its conditions, decline it with
a reason, or simply rule on a matter against it without adopting anything.
The checklist always says which of those is the case.

![One contract type](guide/72-oblik-murabaha.jpg)

### Which type is this draft?

![Comparing types](guide/71-biblioteka-usporedba.jpg)

This is done on **Check a contract**, which the library links to. Pick a type
from the list, or press **compare them all** and Majlis reads the draft
against every type and ranks them. Pressing a ranked row only fills in the
list; it does not choose for you.

### Undertakings

What somebody promised to do, and by when, attached to the matter it came

Part of the board, given a question to look at first. What they found comes
back as a panel on the matter itself.

### Sittings

![Sittings](guide/20-sjednice.jpg)

The agenda, who attended, what was minuted, what was decided, and the board
book that goes out beforehand.

## 20 · What Majlis does not do

Stated here in one place, and stated again on the screen where it matters.

- **It does not decide anything.** No calculator returns a verdict. No
  reader says a contract is compliant. The assistant explains mechanics, not
  permissibility.
- **It does not pick a standard for you.** Which standard governs is the
  board's decision, written in the board's own words.
- **Nothing becomes binding by admin.** Changing what a board holds needs a
  ruling, not a setting.
- **It does not sign for the board.** A password identifies; it does not
  attest.
- **It does not stop a transaction** unless the registry is connected, and
  the bottom bar says which.
- **It tells nobody outside** unless email is connected. A notice is written
  and shown on screen; whether it was actually sent is stated, never
  assumed.
- **It does not host the meeting.**
