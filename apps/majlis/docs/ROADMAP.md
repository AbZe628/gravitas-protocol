# Roadmap

The principal risk is not technical. It is that scholars find it burdensome and do not use it, at
which point it is worth nothing regardless of its qualities. This sequence is designed to earn use
before it requires it.

## Stage One — record and comprehension *(this repository)*

The record, the technological briefings, and the assistant. No governance function whatever. Its
purpose is to be useful to a board that has taken on no obligation, and to let us discover what is
unclear before anything depends on clarity.

Built with a single board that already knows us, and shaped by their objections rather than our
assumptions.

## Stage Two — deliberation

Matters, briefs, simulation and threaded discussion. Votes are recorded but signing remains outside
the system. The full process is exercised with nothing yet resting on it.

Adds: persistent store, proposal lifecycle, roles, notification.

## Stage Three — the vote becomes the signature

Signing authority moves into Majlis. This is the point at which it becomes a governance instrument,
and it should not be reached until a board has used the earlier stages long enough to trust them.

Adds: threshold signing, timelock display with countdown, objection during timelock, emergency
restriction path.

**Key management is the part most likely to fail, and it fails for human rather than technical
reasons.** A senior scholar will not carry a hardware device and will not manage a seed phrase, and
should not be asked to. The approach is threshold cryptography with authentication through means
already familiar to him. No seed phrase is ever displayed, because a secret that must be written
down is a secret that will be written down badly.

Succession, prolonged absence, device loss and quorum change each need a defined answer *before* the
first board is convened. A board that cannot act is a worse outcome than the one it was protecting
against.

## Stage Four — plurality

Multiple boards, multiple institutions, cross-reference between them, and the published reference
that follows from the accumulated record.

Plurality must be assumed from the first line of code rather than accommodated later. Systems which
assume a single truth cannot be persuaded to hold several; they can only be rewritten. Stage One
already carries `boardId` on every rule and matter for this reason.

## Open questions

These are unresolved and are the reason the concept was circulated before anything was built.

1. Where a ruling must be given a precision it did not originally require — a threshold, a
   measurement date, the treatment of a temporary breach — is supplying that precision an act of
   ijtihad belonging to the board, or an operational matter that does not?
2. Should a scholar's signature be personal or institutional? Personal authority follows the man and
   is clearer; institutional authority survives him and is more robust.
3. Does holding operative signing authority alter the nature of a board member's responsibility in a
   way that should be addressed before rather than after the fact?
4. What is the correct minimum deliberation period?
5. Should dissenting reasoning be visible to institutions and the public, or retained within the
   board? Openness serves the record; discretion may serve candour.
